Privacy policy

Last updated: 6 August 2026

Mirra is a workspace for keeping client records. This page explains what happens to your data — and, more to the point, what does not.

The short version

Your records do not pass through us. The app runs entirely in your browser: data travels between your device and Google directly, with nothing in between.

Mirra is accountless: it creates no accounts, keeps no user database, and does not know who is using it.

What Mirra stores about you

Nothing on any server of ours, because there is no such server. In your browser it keeps only what it needs to work:

  • Your access key from Google — so you are not asked to sign in every time. Google voids it after about an hour, after which Mirra quietly obtains a new one. The key stays in your browser and is sent nowhere.
  • Which spreadsheet you chose — its identifier, so the app knows where to look next time.
  • Your settings — theme, language, date format.

The first is held in your browser. The others live in a small file called mirra.json in a folder named Mirra on your own Google Drive — where you can open it, read it, or delete it.

What Mirra can reach on your Drive

Only the files you hand it yourself, through Google's own file picker, plus files it created for you. Everything else on your Drive is invisible to it — not as a matter of policy, but because the permission it holds does not extend that far.

The technical name for that permission is drive.file. It is the narrowest one Google offers for this purpose.

What the site you visit does

Mirra is hosted on ordinary web hosting, and its role is limited: it serves your browser the pages, styles and code of the app — the same for every visitor. Everything after that happens in your browser.

Neither your records nor your access key pass through that server. It does not see them and could not store them even if we wanted it to.

Like any web server, the host technically sees that a page was requested — an IP address and a browser type. We do not collect, analyse or use that.

If you would rather not depend even on that, Mirra's code is open and the app can be run on your own computer or server. Instructions are on GitHub.

What Mirra does not do

  • No analytics, counters, advertising cookies or tracking of any kind
  • No selling of data — there is nothing to sell
  • No passing data to third parties — there is nothing to pass

When new features arrive

In time Mirra will work with other Google services — calendar, mail, maps. The rule will not change: the narrowest permission that does the job, and nothing beyond it. Each new access is requested separately, and you can withdraw it whenever you like at your Google account settings.

Children

Mirra is not intended for children and collects no data about them — nor about anybody else.

Some people use Mirra to keep records that mention minors, such as a client's child. Those records are yours, they live on your Drive, and it is for you to obtain whatever consent your work requires.

Deleting your data

Delete the spreadsheet and the Mirra folder from your Google Drive, and withdraw the app's access at your account permissions. Nothing remains anywhere else, because it never was anywhere else.

Changes to this policy

If it changes, the date at the top changes with it. Anything that affects what Mirra may reach will be asked about separately, in the app.

Getting in touch

Mirra is not a Google product. It uses Google's interfaces to work with files in your own account, and it is governed by Google's own privacy policy where their services are concerned.
