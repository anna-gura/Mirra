The idea

Mirra exists so that you work with people, not with spreadsheets.

Working with clients is two different things. The first is the work itself: the haircut, the lesson, the session, the conversation. The second is everything around it — writing it down, remembering, finding it again, calling back, not forgetting.

The second part is necessary, but it should not eat your evenings. Mirra's job is to make it take as little time as possible.

Who it is for

Anyone who works with people and keeps notes about them.

Once there are more clients than memory holds, you need somewhere everything is written down — and somewhere you can look at any moment.

What it does today

  • Your client records, always at hand Not only at a desk. Open your phone and everything is there.
  • Find anyone in seconds Start typing a name or a number and the right person appears.
  • Call or message in one tap Straight from a client's card, with no copying of numbers.
  • Write it down there and then The client is still in front of you and the record is already made. No "I'll add it later".

As few taps as possible

Mirra does not try to do everything. It hides what you do not need.

Serious business software asks you to learn it first. Fill in fields that mean nothing to you. Work out which of the sections you will never open.

This works the other way round. What is on the screen is what you use every day, and it is one or two taps away.

Your records stay yours

Your records do not pass through Mirra. They travel from your device straight to Google — the app is not an intermediary and keeps nothing of its own.

There is no registration either: you sign in with your Google account, and Mirra creates no account and stores no data about you.

The site you open Mirra from serves only the app itself — pages and code, the same for everyone. Everything after that happens in your browser, between you and Google.

Your spreadsheet stays an ordinary file on your own Google Drive. Open it yourself at a computer and it is all there. Stop using Mirra and your records do not go anywhere.

Only what you allow

Mirra sees only the files you hand it yourself. The rest of your Drive does not exist to it — it does not know what is there and cannot open it.

In time Mirra will work with other Google services too — calendar, mail, maps. The rule will not change: the narrowest permission possible, and nothing beyond it. Each new access is granted separately, and you can withdraw it whenever you like.

The details are in the privacy policy.

What it costs

Nothing. There is no registration either — you sign in with your Google account.

Open source

All of Mirra's code is open. That means two things.

First, anybody can look at exactly what the app does with your data — not take our word for it, but check. Second, Mirra can be run by you: on your own computer or server, under your own domain, depending on nobody.

How to do that is described in the instructions on GitHub.
